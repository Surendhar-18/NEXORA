# ADR 0006: Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop Alert Review

**Status:** Accepted  
**Date:** 2026-10-09  
**Decision-Makers:** Core Architecture Team  

---

## Context
Computer vision algorithms are prone to occasional detection noise, occlusion artifacts, and lighting variations. In high-stakes crowd safety environments, triggering automated mass evacuations or siren alarms based solely on unverified AI model outputs can provoke catastrophic false alarms, stampedes, and operational panic.

## Decision
We mandate a **strict human-in-the-loop triage pattern**: all automated crowd alerts generated by VisionX rules are marked as `pending_review` and **require explicit operator evaluation (Accept or Dismiss)** before any formal incident is created or response resources are dispatched.

## Alternatives Considered
1. **Fully Autonomous Incident Dispatch:** Automatically creating high-priority emergency incidents and triggering simulated dispatch the moment YOLO person count exceeds the threshold.
2. **Advisory Warning Only (No Workflow):** Showing floating banner warnings without an enforceable review queue or tracking mechanism.

## Rationale
- **Life Safety & Stampede Risk:** False alarms in densely packed crowds cause rapid psychological panic. A human operator must visually verify the annotated evidence frame to confirm whether a surge is genuine or an artifact (e.g. umbrellas, banners, optical glare).
- **Responsible AI Principles:** Autonomous decision-making in life-critical contexts violates responsible AI governance guidelines. Keeping a qualified human operator accountable preserves moral agency and system trust.
- **Auditability:** Every operator triage action (Accept / Dismiss) requires an operator note and is recorded in the immutable audit log with timestamp and actor attribution.

## Consequences
- **Positive:** High trust, elimination of automated false-alarm panic, compliance with responsible AI ethics, full audit accountability.
- **Negative:** Introduces an operational verification step (requires human attention during monitoring).
